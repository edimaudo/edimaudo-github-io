Be realistic about the timeline: Three weeks builds the foundation. It does not shorten Ontario's waiting periods: 12 months at G1 (8 with an MTO-approved driver education course), then 12 months at G2, before the final G road test. Confirm rules, fees and booking at drivetest.ca and ontario.ca. If your friend already drove abroad, different rules may apply.

1. The road to a full G

Stage What you must do Limits
G1 knowledge test Vision test plus 40 questions: 20 road signs, 20 rules of the road. Pass mark: 16 of 20 in each section Retakes cost extra
G1 licence Drive only with a fully licensed driver beside you who has 4+ years of experience Zero blood alcohol. No driving midnight to 5 a.m. No 400-series highways or high-speed expressways (401, 404, QEW, DVP, Gardiner) unless an Ontario-licensed driving instructor is with you. Everyone belted
G2 road test (first exit) Basic skills: turns, stops, parking, 3-point turn, residential driving After 12 months at G1 (8 with approved course)
G2 licence Drive alone on all roads Zero blood alcohol. 12 months before the G test. Extra passenger and time rules for drivers 19 and under
G road test (final exit) Advanced skills including highway driving, about 30 minutes DriveTest currently runs a modified G test that omits parallel parking, roadside stops, 3-point turns and residential driving because the G2 test covers them. Confirm the format when you book

Supervisor checklist (that's you): a full G licence held 4+ years, blood alcohol under 0.05 (zero if you're 21 or under), front passenger seat, and your friend added to the car's insurance as a learner.

2. How to run the course

  • 3 to 5 sessions a week, 45 to 90 minutes each, plus 20 minutes of daily sign and rules study. Short and frequent beats long and rare.
  • Each session: brief (5 min), drive, debrief (10 min): one thing that went well, one to fix.
  • A drill is passed when she does it three times in a row with no coaching. Tick it off.
  • Supervisor rule: one calm instruction at a time, given early ("left at the next light"). Don't grab the wheel unless it's an emergency.
  • Where: empty lots (school or church lots on weekends) and quiet side streets first. Avoid rush hour until week 3. Stay on roads below 80 km/h.

3. Coaching hacks that speed things up

  1. Commentary driving. She narrates hazards aloud 5 to 10 seconds ahead ("cyclist on right, car door opening"). It sharpens scanning and shows the examiner awareness.
  2. Exaggerated head checks. Turn the chin visibly toward every mirror and over the shoulder. A check the examiner can't see may not count.
  3. Auto Hold as training wheels. Keep Auto Hold on in week 1 to remove hill-rollback stress, then switch it off in week 2 so she learns natural brake-to-gas control. Either is allowed. This is a teaching choice only.
  4. Reference points beat guesswork. Use cones or bins in a lot to learn where the car's corners are. If you use tape on the windshield as a sight line, remove it before driving on streets and before the test, since examiners check for view obstructions.
  5. Missed a turn? Stay safe. Carry on and reroute. A calm reroute beats a last-second lane dive, which is a real safety fault.
  6. Repeat one route until it's easy, then add one new element. Park in the same spot ten times in a row.
  7. Stress rehearsal. Run mock tests when she's tired or hurried, so test-day nerves feel familiar.
  8. Keep a log: date, minutes, conditions, one lesson learned.

Week 1: Car control and the rules

Goal: know the car, move smoothly, stop precisely, learn the signs.

Session 1: Cockpit and creeping (parking lot)

  • Complete the pre-drive routine in the Orientation Manual
  • Smooth stops: ease off the brake slightly in the last metre so passengers don't head-bob. Imagine an egg under the pedal
  • Steering: hands at 9 and 3; practise both push-pull and hand-over-hand turns
  • Wide turns, then 90° turns using parking lines

Session 2: Residential streets

  • Full stops behind the white line, every time, with the car visibly rocking to rest
  • Left and right turns in the correct lane, no wide swings or cut corners
  • Scanning: eyes 10 to 12 seconds ahead, mirrors every 5 to 8 seconds, shoulder check before every move

Session 3: Intersections and 3-point turn

  • Right of way at 4-way stops and uncontrolled intersections, pedestrian crossovers, yellow-light decisions
  • 3-point turn on a quiet street: signal right, pull over and stop; signal left, check both ways, turn left across to the far curb; stop; reverse turning right; stop; drive away straight. Check for traffic before each move
  • Reverse into a bay using mirrors and a shoulder check first. Use the backup camera only as a secondary aid (confirm its use with your DriveTest centre)

Week 1 study: all road signs by shape, colour and meaning. Then take a full 40-question practice test.

Week 1 gate: smooth stop, correct stop-sign procedure 3 times in a row, 36/40 on a practice test (aim for 90% so test-day nerves don't sink you).

Week 2: Toronto traffic and parking

Goal: handle real city conditions and the G2 test manoeuvres.

Session 4: Arterials and lane changes (off-peak, about 10 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.)

  • Lane change: Mirror, Signal, Blind spot, Go. Adjust speed before the change and avoid braking mid-change
  • Following distance: at least 2 seconds, 4 or more in rain, snow or behind trucks
  • Streetcars: stop at least 2 m behind open streetcar doors. Bike lanes: shoulder-check for cyclists before every right turn
  • Unprotected left turns: enter the intersection with wheels straight, yield to oncoming traffic, scan crosswalks, then go. After a safe gap, move ahead within 4 to 5 seconds

Session 5: Parking (lot first, then street)

  • Parallel parking: start beside the front car, reverse to a 45° angle, straighten, finish close and parallel to the curb. A common instructor target is about 30 cm; this is not an official published number
  • Hill parking: uphill with a curb, turn the wheels away from the curb (left). Downhill, turn them toward the curb (right). Always apply the parking brake
  • Roadside stop: signal, pull right, stop, shoulder check, pull away
  • Perpendicular and angle parking for Green P lots

Session 6: Hazards and conditions

  • School and community safety zones (obey posted limits; fines are higher), construction, emergency vehicles (pull right and stop), pedestrians
  • Rain driving: headlights on, longer gaps, gentle inputs
  • Hold a steady speed within about 5 km/h of the limit using quick glances at the speedometer

Week 2 study: rules of the road (right of way, demerit points, impairment and distraction). Take 2 timed practice tests.

Week 2 gate: parallel park in 3 attempts or fewer, lane change with proper checks 5 of 5, mirror scan on a steady rhythm.

Week 3: Higher speed and mock tests

Goal: build speed and judgement safely, then rehearse the real test.

G1 rule that changes this week: you can't take a G1 driver onto the 401, 404, DVP, QEW or Gardiner. Do the high-speed lesson with an Ontario-licensed driving instructor (they're permitted on 400-series roads), and save independent highway practice for G2.

Session 7: Higher-speed roads

  • 60 to 70 km/h multi-lane arterials (check the posted limit): smooth acceleration, merging from side streets, holding a gap
  • Night driving before midnight and wet-pavement driving: look toward the right edge line to handle oncoming glare
  • Instructor lesson booked for 400-series merging (see below)

Session 8: Mock road test #1 (silent)

  • 30 minutes. Give only neutral directions ("turn left at the next street") and no coaching
  • Include turns, a lane change, a parallel park, a 3-point turn and a roadside stop
  • Score on a sheet, then debrief out of the car

Session 9: Mock road test #2

  • Different route, busier time
  • Zero right-of-way errors, visible shoulder checks every time
  • Write a G2 practice plan (section 6)

Week 3 gate: two 90%+ written practice tests, a mock test with no dangerous actions, and no missed shoulder checks.

What an instructor teaches for 400-series driving

Use the whole acceleration lane to match the flow of traffic (up to the posted limit) before merging. Keep at least a 3-second gap. Pass on the left, then return to the right lane once you can see the passed car's headlights in your mirror.

4. Test-day checklist (verified with DriveTest)

The examiner checks the vehicle first. If something is wrong, your test is declared out-of-order, and you lose 50% of the road-test fee.

  • Both brake lights work, and so do the turn signals, headlights, tail lights and horn
  • Speedometer works, the foot brake works, and the seatbelts work (yours and the examiner's)
  • Wipers and washer fluid, and the defroster, if the weather needs them
  • Windshield free of large cracks or damage
  • Tires in good condition (no flat, no temporary spare). No unsecured parts (hood, plate)
  • No passengers or pets. Phone off. Dashcams and phone recorders disabled
  • Car is clean and fume-free
  • Bring: licence, appointment confirmation, glasses if required, and the vehicle's ownership and insurance documents
  • Arrive 30 minutes early. Call the DriveTest centre beforehand if you're unsure about anything

5. What examiners watch for

Behaviour Done right
Observation Mirrors, then a visible shoulder check before every lane change, turn and pull-away
Stops Complete stop behind the line, with no roll
Speed Steady at the limit. Too slow causes problems for others
Space 2-second gap, an escape route
Signals Early and cancelled
Parking Controlled, in few moves, using observation

Common fails: rolling stops, missed shoulder checks, wide turns, late braking, hesitating, drifting out of the lane.

6. After three weeks: the practice plan to G

Phase Focus Goal
Rest of G1 (8 to 12 months) Short daily drives, then rush hour, winter and night (before midnight). One instructor lesson on 400-series roads 30+ hours logged. Book the G2 test early, since Toronto slots fill quickly
Early G2 Highways with a calm approach: merging, passing, exiting, 100 km/h pace. Start off-peak Calm at 100+ km/h
Last 3 to 4 months of G2 Bad weather, night, mock G tests. You'll be asked to declare highway experience, and DriveTest can cancel the test for too little, so drive highways regularly Book the G test

G test highlights: entering a highway, merging at speed, lane changes at speed, passing, holding speed, exiting safely, and consistent head checks.

Safety reminder: If she is frozen, panicking or tired, pull over safely and end the lesson. Another day is better than a bad habit.
