Read this first: Trims vary (2.0L, 2.5L or 2.5 Turbo; automatic or manual; some features are trim-specific). This guide covers what the 2022 Mazda3 generally shares. For exact specs use the printed owner's manual or the digital one at mazda.ca, and read the tire placard in the driver's door jamb.

1. Cockpit setup (two minutes, every drive)

  • Walk around: tires look even, nothing behind or under the car, lights and windows clear
  • Seat: knees keep a slight bend with the brake fully pressed. Hips back, shoulders against the seatback
  • Steering wheel: pull the column out and tilt it until your wrists rest on top of the rim without lifting your shoulders
  • Interior mirror: frame the whole rear window
  • Side mirrors (blind-spot setting): lean toward the driver window and adjust until the side of your car just disappears. Lean toward the centre and repeat on the right. You must still shoulder-check
  • Seatbelt on, every passenger belted. Phone away, since handheld use is illegal in Ontario

2. Controls

Starting and moving

Action How
Start (automatic) Fob inside, press the brake, press START/STOP
Start (manual) Press the clutch fully, then START/STOP
Gears (automatic) P park, R reverse, N neutral, D drive. Some trims have M (manual mode)
Parking brake Electric: pull the switch beside the shifter up to apply. Press it down with your foot on the brake to release
Auto Hold Holds the car at a stop without your foot on the brake, which is useful on hills and in heavy traffic. You can switch it off, for example when practising slow parking
Turn off Stop, shift to P, press START/STOP

Don't panic: i-stop shuts the engine off at a stop when you hold the brake and restarts when you lift. That's normal and not a stall.

Safety technology (i-ACTIVSENSE)

Feature What it does
Smart Brake Support Warns, and may brake, if you approach something too fast
Blind Spot Monitoring Light in the side mirror when something is beside you
Rear Cross Traffic Alert Beeps when backing out and traffic approaches
Lane Departure Warning / Lane-keep Assist Warns or nudges if you drift without signalling
Radar Cruise Control Holds speed and a gap behind traffic

Rule: These are assistants, not a chauffeur. Do your own shoulder checks and learn to drive without relying on them. The road test grades your head checks.

3. Dashboard warning lights

Lights flash briefly at startup. One that stays on is a message.

Colour Light Meaning What to do
Red Oil can Low oil pressure Pull over safely and shut the engine off
Red Coolant temperature high Overheating Pull over, engine off. Never open a hot radiator cap
Red Battery Charging fault Switch off non-essentials (heated seats, climate, audio) and go straight to a repair shop. Stop if other warnings appear or power drops
Red Brake system, seatbelt, airbag Safety system fault or reminder Stop safely and check. Don't keep driving with a brake warning
Amber Check engine Emissions or engine sensor fault Solid: check the fuel cap, book service. Flashing: reduce speed and load, get service promptly
Amber Tire pressure (horseshoe with !) Low pressure, common in cold snaps Check cold tires against the door-jamb placard (listed in kPa and PSI)
Amber ABS Anti-lock braking fault Brakes still work normally, but book service
Amber, flashing Car with skid marks (DSC/traction) The system is working on a slippery road Ease off the throttle
Blue High beam On Dip for oncoming traffic
Green / white i-ACTIVSENSE, i-stop, cruise System on Nothing needed

5. Under the hood

Park on level ground with the engine off. Check oil about 5 to 10 minutes after shutting down a warm engine. Check coolant only when cold.

Opening it: pull the hood-release lever near your left knee, then slide the safety latch under the front edge of the hood. Lift and secure it with the prop rod. To close, lower it and press down firmly. Never slam it.

# Item How to check
1 Engine oil dipstick Wipe, reinsert fully, pull. Oil sits between the two marks. Canadian Mazda3 engines use 0W-20 oil
2 Coolant reservoir (translucent tank) Level between the LOW and FULL marks, cold only
3 Brake fluid reservoir (near the driver-side firewall) Close to MAX. A noticeable drop suggests worn pads or a leak, so see a mechanic
4 Washer fluid (blue cap) Use winter-rated fluid (typically rated to -40°C). Summer fluid can freeze
5 12V battery Terminals tight, no white or blue corrosion. Mazda says to have a dealer replace it. Sub-zero mornings stress it
6 Fuse boxes One in the engine bay, one low on the driver's side near the left knee. Spare fuses are in the lid

Follow the maintenance schedule in the owner's manual. Short trips and stop-and-go city driving mean shorter oil-change intervals.

6. Road-test morning vehicle check (verified with DriveTest)

The examiner inspects the car first. If something fails, the test is declared out-of-order and you lose 50% of the fee.

  • Both brake lights and the centre light work. Turn signals, headlights and tail lights work. Horn works
  • Speedometer and foot brake work
  • Seatbelts work, for you and the examiner. Both doors open and close
  • Wipers and washer fluid, and the defroster, if the weather calls for them
  • Windshield free of large cracks or damage
  • Tires in good condition. No flat, no temporary spare. Hood and plate secure
  • No passengers or pets. Dashcams and phone recorders disabled
  • Car clean, no fumes or fuel leaks
  • Ownership and insurance documents are in the car. Arrive 30 minutes early

7. Toronto know-how

  • Winter: brush off the whole car, roof included, and clear every window. Keep a scraper, small shovel, blanket and booster cables in the trunk.
  • Road salt: wash the underbody monthly in winter.
  • Potholes (Feb to Apr): slow down early and don't brake hard over one. Check tire pressure after a hard hit.
  • Streetcars and TTC: learn the car's corners in a lot before tight downtown streets.
  • Parking: read every sign. Green P lots and street parking differ. A photo of the sign helps in disputes.
  • Cyclists: open your door with your far hand ("Dutch reach") so you look back.

8. Quick reference card

Situation Action
Red warning light Signal, pull over safely, stop, call roadside assistance
Flat or TPMS alarm Slow gently, stop in a safe spot, hazards on, use the repair kit or spare per your manual
Dead battery Follow the jump-start steps in the owner's manual or call roadside assistance
Collision Move to safety if possible, hazards on, call 911 for injuries, exchange information, report as required
Stuck in snow Straighten the wheels, clear around the tires, rock gently between D and R

Save in your phone: your insurer's claims line, a roadside assistance number (Mazda or CAA), and a link to the digital owner's manual.
